# Operating and maintaining a deployed application

The Project Manager coordinates maintenance. Each deployed application has a named support owner, backup and an authorised production operator. The support owner is accountable for the service, including coverage gaps. The PM owns the maintenance queue and escalation. Record the people or staffed services that actually hold those responsibilities, their coverage hours and how to reach them. An agent session can assist when invoked. It does not provide monitoring, on-call coverage or authority to change production.

## Accept the support handoff

1. Before asking for production release approval, copy `starter/templates/maintenance-record.md` into the named project's `maintenance/` folder. Record the service, environments, critical user journeys, support owner and backup, operator, expected support coverage, post-deploy verification deadline with time zone, incident and escalation routes, and approval boundaries. Link the release and rollback plans and a current runbook. Record evidence, or a reason with approver for any control that does not apply. Missing required readiness remains open at the release gate.
2. Verify the actual tools and routes. Rehearse delivery and acknowledgement of a synthetic alert with the support owner and backup. Verify the applicable health signals and the recovery procedure, including a backup restore rehearsal in an authorised isolated environment for data you are responsible for recovering. An enabled backup job only demonstrates that the job is configured or ran. Record restore evidence separately. Use existing approved tools and permissions; record a setup task for missing capabilities.
3. After deployment, the authorised operator records the actual live revision, time, and result. QA or the authorised operator verifies production-safe smoke checks and reads the specified monitoring signals. Record the time window and expected versus observed behaviour. Assign and verify follow-up if evidence is missing. The support owner records acceptance before the handoff is complete. Keep the project in active maintenance once accepted. If the handoff is blocked, record the true live status and interim contact. The PM escalates to the CEO immediately if a support owner or critical control is unavailable, and at the recorded verification deadline if any other issue still blocks acceptance. For an already-live application with a blocked handoff and no recorded deadline, escalate when the gap is found.

## Plan recurring maintenance

Set cadence and evidence requirements with the service owner for each project. Use the recorded support hours and approved targets; do not invent an uptime, response or recovery guarantee. Track the next due date, last actual run and result, and owner for each check. Mark missed or unconfigured checks explicitly. Where applicable, schedule:

- Service availability and important user workflows, error rates, latency, capacity, alert delivery and integration health.
- Backup status, retention against the approved plan and periodic restore rehearsals. Validate recovery time and possible data loss against approved targets when those targets exist.
- Dependency and security updates, expiring certificates, credentials held in approved stores, and vendor changes. Escalate any permissions or privacy issue to the appropriate specialist.
- Maintenance backlog, recurring incidents, support costs, and documentation and runbook validity. Reprioritise planned maintenance with the PM when it affects budget or the single developer pair.

The support owner records what actually ran. Bring material support risks and a decision request to the CEO through the PM. Set up scheduled checks in the project's approved operating tools when the schedule, route and owner are known.

Use the approved cost plan to track utilisation, quota changes, renewed commitments and forecast cash exposure. Route scope or spend decisions through the PM before authorising new commitments.

## Temporary pause of the active build

The CEO may pre-authorise the PM and a named backup in versioned Markdown to pause the current build when a stated critical-incident threshold is met. The portfolio records the exact approval, qualifying threshold, scope, maximum duration, escalation route and restart authority. The available delegate checks those conditions, records the incident and checkpoint, pauses the affected build slot, and notifies the CEO promptly through the approved route. Log notification attempts and use the approved fallback if delivery fails. Only separately authorised runbook triage and containment can proceed. A pause grants no spending, reassignment or Production action.

If delegation is absent, expired, conflicting or out of scope, use the existing escalation path, record the blocker and seek the CEO decision for a build-slot pause. Existing safety stops and separately authorised containment still apply. At the recorded deadline, route unresolved state to the CEO and let the pause authority expire. The named restart authority confirms the criteria in the approval and records the decision before work resumes. An extension or permanent reprioritisation requires the CEO's recorded decision.

## Handle an incident

1. **Record and assess.** Create an incident ID in the project maintenance record. Capture time with zone, environment and affected revision, observed symptom, impact or uncertainty, severity, owner, and evidence. Use the severity rules in `workflows/PEER-REVIEW.md`. Avoid exposing secrets or unnecessary live data. A suspected critical incident is escalated immediately under the charter. Record who was notified, whether they acknowledged it and when the next update is due. If the PM is unavailable, follow the agreed fallback; record pending delivery if the route is unavailable.
2. **Restore within authority.** The designated operator may execute a previously authorised runbook action only within its documented conditions, permissions and rollback boundaries. If the approval or applicable runbook is absent, seek the human decision and record the alert. Triage and recovery require approved Markdown covering the action. Record the action, scope, approval reference and actual result. The PM runs triage and escalation in parallel with approved work where possible, and obtains any required priority or budget decision before switching the build slot. An urgent incident does not grant authority to deploy new code.
3. **Fix and verify.** Link the permanent fix to the incident. Include the exact candidate, independent Codex and Claude Code peer review, QA on the integrated candidate, specialist review where required, documentation updates, rollback plan and CEO production approval. The authorised operator records deployment. Confirm restoration from production-safe checks and the relevant signals over the recorded observation window. Use `Awaiting verification` if the evidence is incomplete; keep recovery status separate from root-cause analysis and follow-up work.
4. **Close and learn.** For critical and high incidents, record the timeline, verified root cause or remaining hypotheses, user impact, mitigation, validation, prevention tasks, owners and dates. The Technical Writer updates affected runbooks, troubleshooting notes, release notes or support guides and records the version. The PM confirms incident disposition and routes any unresolved follow-up into the maintenance backlog. Customer communications follow the project's approved authority and channels.

Support may end after a named successor accepts the handoff or the CEO approves retirement. Record the decision, unresolved obligations and how users and data are handled under the approved plan. Technical readiness evidence and operating records stay in the project's named folder.
