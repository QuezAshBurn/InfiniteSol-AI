# Requirements improvements and business opportunities

Ask whether the requested solution achieves the intended user outcome. Surface useful alternatives during discovery, after meaningful feedback and when live service data reveals a problem. The Business Analyst coordinates the requirement review. Anyone can raise a proposal with evidence. The PM owns disposition and the link to any approved decision.

## Classify the proposal

| Type | What the team examines | Who contributes |
|---|---|---|
| Requirement clarification or fix | Missing rules, exceptions, accessibility, conflicting expectations or an acceptance criterion the team cannot verify. | BA records current and intended behaviour. Architect and QA check feasibility and testability. |
| Product or process improvement | Rework, waiting, manual steps, recurring errors, adoption friction, or a simpler way to deliver the same outcome. | BA establishes the baseline. Architect checks cost and risk. QA defines verification. Finance checks material cost or benefit assumptions. |
| Business opportunity | An underserved segment, adjacent workflow, integration, paid add-on, service or repeatable product. | Marketing tests the buyer, problem, alternatives and demand. Finance tests economics and support costs. BA and Architect assess fit and feasibility. Security, Legal and other specialists join when the project triggers apply. |

A finding can be both an improvement and a commercial idea. Give each decision its own evidence and scope. Flag urgent defects through the project or maintenance incident route even if an improvement proposal also exists.

## From observation to decision

1. **Record.** Assign an `OPP-###` ID within the project. Link the original requirement, source evidence or service signal. State the affected users or buyer, observed baseline and problem. Label estimates, assumptions, conflicting evidence and unknowns. Keep this separate from what the team is approved to build.
2. **Compare.** Include a reasonable baseline option such as retaining the current process, a simple process or configuration alternative where viable, and a build option where justified. Compare expected value, strategic fit, validation time and cost, implementation and support burden, risks, and what would be displaced. State the assumptions that could reverse the recommendation.
3. **Validate.** The PM records the owner, authority, cost or time limit, evidence source, success threshold and stop rule. A small read-only check may proceed within the existing discovery allowance. External outreach, extra spending, live changes, or other work outside existing authority require a separate decision beforehand. An approved experiment authorises only that experiment. Return the observed result, sample limitations and contrary evidence. Keep benefits labelled as hypotheses or forecasts until measured.
4. **Decide and trace.** Recommend clarify, validate, defer, drop, or propose a backlog or scope change. Record who decided and why. The PM can route a clarification within existing authority. Preserve the approved requirement, baseline and original evidence until a change is authorised. Material scope, budget, priority or release changes go to the CEO under the charter. Link the separate implementation decision, approved baseline revision and acceptance criteria before the team builds. Recheck measured benefits after delivery when evidence is available.

Use the project-local `improvement-opportunity.md` record. Review the ideas that could change a decision. Keep the rest in the register with a clear reason to defer or close them.

## Worked example | hypothetical

A distributor asks for a low-stock dashboard. The BA first checks whether the practical problem is identifying low stock or getting a timely purchasing decision. Compare the dashboard, a daily exception list, and the current process. Measure how long the buyer takes to identify and act on a shortage using authorised evidence. The possible business opportunity is an optional replenishment add-on for distributors with the same problem. Marketing tests whether that problem and willingness to pay exist. Finance tests the expected cost to build and support it. Until verified, those benefits are hypotheses and the dashboard scope remains as approved.
